# Dimensions and measuring

## Written dimensions (cotes)

- A written dimension is the designer's value: quote it as written, with its
  unit (usually cm or m on plans, mm on details) and what it measures (axis to
  axis, finished face to finished face, opening in the rough or finished).
- Dimension chains add up: when a total is asked, add the partial dimensions
  written, or quote the overall dimension when written; say which.
- Heights on sections and elevations are often altimetry values (+2,50):
  compute a height as the difference of two written levels and say so.

## Measuring on the drawing (only when no written dimension answers)

1. Check that the drawing does not say « ne pas mesurer » or « cotes à vérifier
   sur place »; if it does, do not measure.
2. Read the scale of the view (title block or under the view; a sheet may hold
   views at several scales).
3. Check the scale on a written dimension of the same view: measure it and
   compare. If they differ by more than a few percent, the PDF was resized:
   use the ratio found, or do not measure.
4. Measure, round to a sensible precision (about 5 cm at 1/100, 1 cm at 1/50),
   give the uncertainty and mark the value **déduit (mesuré à l'échelle)**.

## Never

- Measure when a written dimension exists.
- Present a measured value as written.
- Measure a scanned or photographed drawing without a written dimension to
  check the scale on.
