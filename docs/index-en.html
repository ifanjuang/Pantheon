You already use AI.

Who answers for what it writes?

An email to a client drafted in thirty seconds. A specification summarised while you are on the phone. A note re-read between two meetings. You are not a heavy user: you open it when it saves you an hour.

And every time, it is your name at the bottom of the page. The answer is fluent, polite, plausible. Nobody tells you what it leaned on, what it assumed in order to fill the gaps, or what it has just made you accept.

A better model would not have known either. Nor would a better question. The problem is not what the AI can do: it is what the AI received.

One case · Tuesday, 6.40 pm

“Can you answer this email for me?”

Mme. C asks whether the ventilation item is closed, so that handover can start. You paste the thread into your assistant, along with the latest technical note from record LIA21 that you had open. Thirty seconds later: a clear, courteous, professional message. It says yes. You send it.

Four things have just happened. None of them appears in the message.

01

The exhibit declared; it did not attest.

The note at revision C announces compliance. It carries no test result: it is a declaration, not a finding.

02

An exhibit was missing.

The site report of 6 August left the point open. The assistant did not see it — and could not know that it existed.

03

The message commits you.

It does not answer: it authorises handover to start. Drafting and sending became one gesture, with no stopping point.

04

Nothing flagged it.

No doubt, no caveat, no “I do not have that document”. The tone was exactly the same as for a correct answer.

You did nothing wrong. You passed on what you had to hand — that is normal use, and it is exactly what saves the time. The problem is not in your gesture: it is in what the assistant received, and in what it worked out on its own.

And it is not only you

The same record is already receiving AI through several doors at once.

You use it for an email. Your colleague uses it for a note. Your professional software built it in without asking. And the client, the consulting engineer, or the contractor send you material that has already passed through an AI, without saying so.

Each use is reasonable on its own. Yet they all land in the same record, under the same responsibility — yours. The question is not whether everyone should use the same AI: it is knowing what the record received, and where it came from.

What actually happens

An AI does not guess. It completes from the fragments it is given.

A model does not go looking for what it is missing, and above all: it does not know that anything is missing. It receives an extract with no revision mark, a mail pulled out of its thread, an undated drawing — and it fills the gaps with whatever is most plausible. That is exactly what happened at 6.40 pm.

The result is fluent, coherent, presentable. That is exactly what makes it dangerous: nothing in the form signals that the reconstruction of the record was wrong.

This is not a model defect, it is an input defect. The quality of an answer depends first on how you organised what you handed over.

It fills

A gap does not look like a gap.

It is replaced by the most plausible assumption, without warning, in the middle of a paragraph that is otherwise right.

It does not date

The superseded version weighs as much as the latest.

With no date or revision mark attached to the exhibit, a document replaced six months ago remains a perfectly credible source.

It blends

A rule from another project becomes a rule of yours.

Nothing spontaneously separates what belongs to this record, to another record, to the practice, or to yesterday’s conversation.

It convinces

The form is always good.

The professional tone is constant, whether the substance is established, partial, or invented. The reader is left to tell the difference.

In a trade where you answer for your signature, a brilliant but wrong answer is not a mere bug. It commits your liability, it is copied into the next note, and it becomes the version everyone quotes. Speed is already there: what is missing is control.

A professional frame

For professional use of AI, a plausible answer is not enough.

You need a frame that can understand the context in which a question is asked and recognise what is missing. When important information is absent, the system does not silently replace it with an assumption: it looks for it within the authorised scope, asks for the necessary clarification, or explicitly flags the uncertainty.

The answer can then be situated, annotated, and bounded: it distinguishes what is established, what is inferred, what remains hypothetical, and the scope within which it may be used. That is the professional frame Pantheon brings to AI-assisted work.

Gate 1 · entry

Which information may we rely on?

A retrieved source is a candidate, not proof. It keeps its origin, date, revision, status, and scope — or its absence remains visible.

Gate 2 · context

What is missing to answer properly?

The frame first looks within the authorised scope. If material information remains absent or ambiguous, it flags the gap and asks for what is needed instead of silently turning an assumption into fact.

Gate 3 · output

What can we actually say?

The output keeps a status and its support: established, inferred, to be checked, candidate. It states what it rests on, the scope in which it holds, and what remains open.

Gate 4 · memory

What may remain afterwards?

What has been approved remains with its scope and source. The rest may help in the moment without automatically becoming a durable reference.

The same Tuesday, 6.40 pm. The request is recognised for what it is: a commitment to the outside. The note arrives with its revision and status — it declares, it does not attest. The site report of 6 August still leaves the point open. The test report needed to conclude is not available: instead of producing a confident “yes”, the system keeps that gap visible in the answer and asks for the document or clarification required. You can then answer with an explicit caveat, wait for the exhibit, or continue the verification.

What is missing stays visible. What is asserted stays linked to its support and its scope.

Who it is for. For professionals who answer for what they send: architects, lawyers, doctors, accountants, engineers, consultants. Trades where a brilliant but wrong answer is not a mere bug. No technical skill is assumed: you write from your usual channel, and you keep hold of the sources, the statuses, and the signatures.

Semantic continuity of the project

One project exists through several representations at once.

A drawing, an IFC or Revit model, a specification, a photograph, and a site report may refer to the same object without saying exactly the same thing. Pantheon aims to connect them around stable identities and explicit relations without flattening their differences into one value.

Global understanding comes from being able to superimpose the fragments, not from automatically merging them into one truth. For a given question, Hermès can then receive a bounded project neighbourhood and compose relevant consequences while keeping sources, contradictions, potentially applicable requirements, and missing information visible.

Identity

Several sources, the same object.

A wall on a drawing, its IFC occurrence, a specification requirement, and a site observation may be attached to the same governed identity when the match is sufficiently established.

Divergence

Disagreements stay visible.

A drawing may state 100 mm while the model states 120 mm. Pantheon keeps both claims with their provenance; it does not silently choose a truth.

Context

A question does not open the whole project.

Active context stays bounded to the identities and sources admitted for the task. A known relation does not on its own authorise global traversal of the project.

Understanding

Hermès composes; Pantheon does not pre-write the reasoning.

The model may connect space, opening, services, documents, and Knowledge to propose consequences. That interpretation remains distinct from governed claims and professional decision.

What it makes possible

Three capabilities that are hard to sustain without a shared frame.

The point is not to go faster: you already are. It is to be able to rely on what comes out — and to know, without re-reading everything, when you can.

Today · your attention

Not re-reading everything.

If every output has to be re-checked by the only person whose signature is on the line, the time saved evaporates. Or people stop checking — which is worse.

Principle: consequence is qualified before anything may commit the practice. Work with no external effect may flow; consequential work must meet the appropriate exhibit, decision, or authorisation. The executable paths that enforce this are still being qualified step by step in the repository.

Tomorrow · action

Letting AI act without handing it the pen.

Assistants no longer just answer: they send, they file, they write into your tools. Once that reaches the outside, “answering is not acting” becomes a practical safeguard.

Principle: preparing an effect and authorising it remain separate acts. Consequential paths require a human Decision followed by exact verification before execution; operational adoption remains distinct from repository qualification.

In five years · your asset

Owning what the practice learns.

What accumulates today inside conversation threads is neither citable, nor transferable, nor portable. It dies with the subscription, and it leaves with the person.

Principle: the governed register belongs to the project, not to the engine that produced an answer. A person or model may change without automatically turning memory, runtime output, or projection into a durable reference.

And the day you are asked. A client, an insurer, a peer, an expert: “how did AI take part in this record?” is about to become an ordinary question. The defensible answer is not “carefully”. It is a decision path you can show: which exhibits, on which date, under which status, approved by whom.

The objection

“You are adding work to my day.”

That is the first reaction, and it is a fair one. The four gates assume that exhibits carry their origin, their date, their revision, and their scope. If all of that rests on you, the frame costs more than it returns — and nobody will keep it up for three weeks.

So the rule is simple: qualify at the useful moment, once, rather than re-entering the same facts everywhere. This is how the work should divide.

Automatable

What the frame can pick up without re-entry.

A file’s origin, date, version, thread, or project when those metadata already exist. They are surfaced rather than typed again.

Proposed

What it can propose for confirmation.

The status of an exhibit, its scope, whether an answer commits you to the outside. One gesture to confirm, one to correct — when the information is actually used.

Human

What stays your gesture.

Deciding that something becomes a reference, approving what commits you, signing. Those decisions simply become visible, dated, and reviewable.

To avoid

What the frame should not require.

Re-filing the whole history, migrating every document, maintaining a taxonomy by hand, or changing tools before you can begin.

On the current AFFAIRES target, that rule is now concrete. Professional files remain on the NAS and are mounted directly on Linux: the selected path no longer keeps a durable AFFAIRES mirror on the Linux disk and no longer depends on Obsidian, LiveSync or CouchDB. One Workspace daemon maintains a reconstructible technical index and produces Hindsight operations. Hidden Markdown cartouches such as .name.ext.md describe sources without replacing them; a revision relation is recorded only when it is explicitly established, never inferred from a filename, revision label or date.

Where exactly the line falls between these four columns still has to be tested on real records. The point of the first pilots is to find where qualification becomes a reflex rather than a chore. If that line does not exist, that has to be acknowledged.

Architecture · in one minute

Four resources, one frame, one decision: memory or register.

Behind any answer there are always four things: a model, a way of working, some knowledge, and sometimes connected tools. Pantheon names them, situates them, and keeps track of the ones that were really used.

Then a single question matters: does what has just been produced remain working context or runtime memory, or does it become a governed reference the practice can rely on?

AI · models

The language capability.

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, a local model, or the AI built into professional software. They write, compare, and reason — and they remain interchangeable. Runtime memory remains outside Pantheon.

Skills

Bounded ways of working.

Writing a report, comparing two documents, preparing a consultation. A Skill describes how to perform the work correctly with the admitted exhibits and how to return the result; it does not decide the scope or authorise an effect. Available does not mean authorised.

Knowledge · web

What the answer rests on.

Project documents, practice knowledge, and the web when the question calls for it. Each element keeps its origin, date, and scope: found is not verified.

Connections

The tools the AI can reach.

Mail, storage, professional software, external services. Reading is one thing; writing, sending, or committing the practice is another, and it requires explicit authorisation.

Memory and register

Remembering is not the same as being a reference.

This is one of Pantheon’s most useful distinctions. Teams already work this way without naming it: a recollection of a meeting and a documented decision do not play the same role, even when they say the same thing.

Working memory · Hermes / derived retrieval

It remembers, it proposes.

The conversation thread, runtime state, working habits and, when selected, derived Hindsight recall. This memory may be technically persistent without becoming governed Pantheon state.

  • Useful, fast, replaceable
  • May be native to Hermes or supplied by an external component
  • Carries no authority: nothing it retains justifies a decision on its own
Probative Register · Pantheon side

The register retains what may be cited.

Information does not enter because it is useful or often repeated: it enters because it was proposed as a candidate, qualified, and retained under the required level of review.

  • Source, date, and supporting exhibit
  • Declared scope: project, practice, domain
  • Certainty level, review and withdrawal possible

Between the two is a qualification gate: a recollection does not become a reference through accumulation, repetition, or model confidence.

remembered ≠ authoritativefound online ≠ verifiedconnected ≠ authorised

General example. “The assistant remembers that you prefer concise summaries” belongs to working memory. “A decision was approved on a given date, with a reference exhibit and an explicit scope” may become a register candidate. The first makes work easier; the second has to remain justifiable and reviewable.

Cognitive ecology · Roles · Places · Rites

Not a central brain: an ecology of attention.

Pantheon does not try to make one agent think in place of the professional. It separates forms of judgement and attention that can coexist, disagree, and correct one another without allowing a single voice to absorb the whole record.

To keep the model simple, the reading begins with three questions: who looks at what, in which context, and by which method? Beneath those three readings, objects keep their identity, history, and provenance; rules bound what may be inferred or done.

Roles

Who looks at what?

The same situation may require framing the task, checking sources, spotting a risk, reviewing a version, or preparing a transmission.

Here: those responsibilities stay separate so one smooth answer cannot hide their disagreements.

Rites

How do we check when things become difficult?

Compare sources that diverge. Challenge an answer that sounds too convincing. Widen the options before choosing. Restart from a clean context.

Here: those moves become bounded methods, opened only when they add value.

Places and scopes

In which context may the information be used?

Project information is not automatically a general rule; an internal note is not a decision; a temporary recollection is not a durable reference.

Here: scope stays visible, and reuse outside it has to be justified.

Why call it an ecology? Because the aim is not to multiply agents but to organise an environment: several viewpoints may remain distinct, a contradiction may stay open, a new observation may correct what was previously believed, and none of those movements automatically turns information into truth or authorisation. Objects — source, document, claim, Evidence, decision — keep their own state and history. Contracts, rules, and invariants keep their owners. Hermès executes what has been admitted; the professional decides what commits the practice.

Why Greek names? The function comes before the name. The names are simply short handles for eight distinct responsibilities of judgement. They do not mean eight agents are constantly debating: a Role may stay on standby until its viewpoint is useful. Mnemosyne, for example, matters when previous state, competing versions, or retention become material; Argos remains responsible for the value of the sources. Visible context

See the context you give the AI, not only give it.

An answer depends on the information actually drawn upon. Pantheon therefore makes the active context readable: the record, the documents, the knowledge, the earlier decisions, and, where relevant, the tools involved.

The professional can see what is proposed, what is retained, where it comes from, which scope it belongs to, and which version is in use.

Not all information has the same lifespan. A conversation may stay temporary; an element may belong durably to a project; knowledge may become reusable more widely. Pantheon keeps those transitions situated, traceable, and revisable.

Rites · explicit method

Some situations deserve more than a fast answer.

Two sources contradict each other, an answer sounds too convincing, an old assumption keeps being reused, or the context has become overloaded: these are general situations. A Rite provides a review method bounded to that kind of tension.

A Rite opens only when it adds something, then closes explicitly. It structures the method; it does not replace human decision.

Concordance

Compare the sources.

When several documents do not yet support the same conclusion.

Self-critique

Put a proposal to the test.

When an answer seems convincing enough that it becomes useful to re-read it as if it came from someone else.

Divergence

Widen before converging.

When the problem deserves several avenues before a choice narrows the field too early.

Refoundation

Start again from a clean context.

When a session has accumulated too many assumptions, instructions, or topics to stay reliable.

Design

Designing is not only optimising.

AI can analyse a brief, explore site layouts, compare variants, simulate, calculate, search for references, or propose forms. It considerably widens the field of possibilities.

But a project also brings together a place, uses, resources, a budget, a technique, an existing fabric, a territory, and people. These dimensions can pull against each other: that is where architectural judgement is exercised.

Ecology

Understanding an environment.

Climate, ground, resources, existing fabric, material, uses, and duration go beyond measured performance alone.

Ethics

Asking what it is desirable to do.

A possible solution is not necessarily right for the client, the users, the territory, or those who will build it.

Aesthetics

Giving the project a sensory quality.

Light, proportion, material, relation to landscape, emotion, and architectural culture do not reduce to a score.

An intuition may appear before it can be fully explained. A calculation may call it into question. A proposal generated by an AI may open an unexpected avenue. These can converse without being confused with each other.

Not everything that matters in a project is calculable. And not everything calculable should decide the project.

Pantheon preserves the context and the distinctions that let the architect keep their attention, culture, intuition, and judgement when AI takes part in design.

Keep your tools

A shared frame does not require a single tool.

Your AI assistants, local models, and professional software remain specialised and replaceable. Each keeps its role. Pantheon provides the shared frame that qualifies context, provenance, status, and decisions around the record without becoming the owner of the files or tools.

Hermès

An external, optional execution agent that can mobilise models and tools for a bounded task.

04

Cockpit

The surface where the professional consults the project, its context, its working states, and the decisions that need attention.

Under the hood. The governance layer drafts nothing and sends nothing: it answers as data. For a given request it classifies the consequence, states the verification required, sets the approval ceiling, and names the gates to pass. An action towards the outside is refused by default, with the explicit path to legitimise it. The same bounded service can be consulted through a read-only MCP server and an authenticated internal HTTP API for deterministic preflight. Those transports consult, validate, and verify; they do not execute the action, send anything, or approve anything.

In practice. Models produce, Hermès may execute a bounded task, and the Cockpit makes context, status, and review points visible. Pantheon keeps those responsibilities separate so that a tool able to act cannot, in the same gesture, decide by itself what is true, authorised, or durable. The professional remains responsible for what commits the practice.

Responsibility

Pantheon provides the framework. The practice defines the applicable rules.

Pantheon is not an artificial intelligence model, a professional regulation, or a regulatory authority. It does not on its own guarantee the compliance of a project. The applicable rules, references, methods, limits, and responsibilities remain defined and maintained by the professionals concerned.
