# Practitioner Hook Topics

Status: fictional example planning — educational support only.
Boundary profile: documentation_only.

This document lists practical scenarios that may interest non-technical professionals faster than abstract governance doctrine.

It does not define implementation.

It does not create runtime behavior.

It does not provide legal, medical, technical, accounting, financial or professional advice.

## Core principle

A practitioner becomes interested when Pantheon prevents one of six concrete failures:

```text
1. a fluent AI answer becomes a risky professional act;
2. a source is treated as proof too early;
3. a contradiction disappears into a clean summary;
4. a draft is mistaken for a deliverable;
5. a useful claim becomes memory without scope or approval;
6. new external information contradicts active dossier assumptions.
```

The best examples should therefore show a mistake avoided, not only a document produced.

## Highest-priority demo topics

### 1. Dangerous client email

Professional hook:

```text
The AI writes a polite email that may accidentally validate too much.
```

Best suited professions:

- architect / MOE;
- lawyer;
- consultant;
- accountant;
- project manager.

Pantheon value:

- detects external effect;
- blocks transmission;
- proposes neutral wording;
- preserves evidence gaps;
- asks for human decision.

Why it works:

Most practitioners have already sent or received a message that later mattered more than expected.

### 2. Regulatory watch versus active dossiers

Professional hook:

```text
A new regulation, case law item, official doctrine or standard appears and may contradict assumptions used in active dossiers.
```

Best suited professions:

- architect / MOE;
- lawyer;
- notary;
- doctor;
- accountant;
- DPO / compliance;
- consultant;
- executive.

Pantheon value:

- detects potential contradiction between external update and active dossier assumptions;
- does not mutate the dossier automatically;
- creates an Evidence Candidate or Watch Alert;
- marks affected dossiers as review-needed;
- asks the professional whether to update, ignore, defer or split by date of applicability.

Why it works:

Practitioners fear relying on outdated assumptions.

Pantheon can make freshness risk visible without pretending that every new regulation automatically applies.

Key distinction:

```text
new information ≠ applicable rule
watch alert ≠ dossier update
retrieved regulation ≠ evidence
impact suspected ≠ conclusion
```

### 3. Quote versus specification comparison

Professional hook:

```text
The client wants to know if a quote can be accepted.
```

Pantheon value:

- separates in-scope, out-of-scope, unclear and risky items;
- keeps contradictions visible;
- avoids global approval;
- produces a decision checklist.

Why it works:

It turns AI from a summarizer into a review assistant with boundaries.

### 4. Contradictory dossier synthesis

Professional hook:

```text
The same fact appears differently in emails, reports and attachments.
```

Pantheon value:

- builds a contradiction ledger;
- preserves source versions;
- avoids smoothing conflict;
- proposes questions to resolve.

Why it works:

Professionals often lose time because the problem is not lack of text, but conflict between pieces.

### 5. Missing-source blocker

Professional hook:

```text
The AI can draft the answer, but the decisive source is missing.
```

Pantheon value:

- marks source gap;
- downgrades output to draft;
- blocks delivery when needed;
- asks for a bounded source.

Why it works:

It shows that stopping is sometimes the correct professional output.

### 6. Memory refused

Professional hook:

```text
The user wants the system to remember something, but the claim is only true for one dossier.
```

Pantheon value:

- rejects broad memory;
- proposes scoped Register Candidate;
- requires evidence linkage;
- avoids cross-dossier contamination.

Why it works:

Memory is attractive, but dangerous when context is narrow.

## Profession-specific scenarios

### Architect / MOE

High-impact examples:

- recovery quote after contractor default;
- CCTP versus quote gap analysis;
- new regulation or standard contradicting assumptions in ongoing project dossiers;
- reception and reservations checklist;
- client email that must not imply validation;
- contradictory site minutes versus contractor claim;
- payment request review without implying reception;
- ABF / mairie response requiring source and scope control;
- mission boundary warning when the client asks beyond DET scope.

Strongest hooks:

```text
Pantheon prevents a well-written email from becoming implicit professional validation.
Pantheon flags when a new rule may make an active project assumption obsolete.
```

### Lawyer / legal counsel

High-impact examples:

- strategy note from exhibits and opposing brief;
- new case law contradicting an argument reused in active notes;
- missing exhibit prevents conclusion;
- draft argument with case-law verification required;
- client-facing summary versus internal legal note;
- obligation table from contract and amendments;
- contradictory dates across contract, emails and invoices;
- redaction before external AI use.

Strongest hook:

```text
Pantheon prevents a plausible legal note from hiding unverified sources or outdated authorities.
```

### Doctor / healthcare professional

High-impact examples:

- referral letter from notes and labs;
- new guideline or official recommendation conflicting with a reusable note template;
- pseudonymized external drafting;
- source separation between patient note, lab result and hypothesis;
- clinical decision kept outside AI output;
- missing result blocks final wording;
- specialist question made explicit;
- no durable AI memory for patient identity.

Strongest hook:

```text
Pantheon helps draft without confusing documentary support, current guidance and clinical decision.
```

Medical examples should remain clearly marked as draft/documentary support pending professional consultation.

### Accountant / consultant

High-impact examples:

- client document synthesis with missing invoices;
- new tax, accounting or reporting rule affecting recurring client templates;
- table reconciliation with contradictory amounts;
- management memo with assumptions separated from facts;
- client-facing conclusion blocked until source gap closes;
- recurring client memory scoped to one engagement;
- audit trail for how a synthesis was produced.

Strongest hook:

```text
Pantheon prevents a clean synthesis from hiding unsupported figures or obsolete assumptions.
```

### Notary

High-impact examples:

- missing piece checklist;
- new applicable rule or official position affecting a reusable checklist;
- contradictory civil-status or property documents;
- sale dossier synthesis;
- inheritance dossier chronology;
- client communication that must distinguish explanation and formal advice;
- memory scoped to one estate or transaction.

Strongest hook:

```text
Pantheon keeps missing pieces and outdated assumptions visible instead of letting AI write around them.
```

### DPO / compliance / executive

High-impact examples:

- data-processing register synthesis;
- regulatory watch alert affecting active compliance dossiers;
- DPIA source pack;
- contradictory vendor claims;
- policy update candidate with approval path;
- external tool risk classification;
- AI usage register and evidence-ready audit export.

Strongest hook:

```text
Pantheon turns AI assistance into an audit-ready decision trail and watches for external changes without auto-mutating dossiers.
```

## Cross-cutting subjects to develop

### Before / after comparison

Every demo should show:

```text
raw AI answer
→ what is dangerous or weak
→ Pantheon-framed answer
→ what was blocked, preserved or escalated
```

### Watch alert gallery

Create examples of alerts where new information may affect active dossiers:

- new regulation;
- new case law;
- new official doctrine;
- new technical standard;
- new professional recommendation;
- new contractual clause model;
- new internal office rule.

Each alert should show:

```text
new external source
→ affected assumption
→ affected dossier(s)
→ applicability uncertainty
→ evidence candidate
→ user decision gate
→ update / defer / reject / archive
```

### User Decision Gate gallery

Create several examples of decision gates:

- source missing;
- scope too broad;
- transmission risky;
- memory too broad;
- contradiction unresolved;
- professional risk high;
- external update may contradict active dossier assumption.

### Evidence Pack gallery

Create simplified Evidence Packs for:

- architecture quote review;
- regulatory watch conflict;
- legal strategy note;
- medical referral letter;
- accountant reconciliation note;
- compliance memo.

### Register Candidate gallery

Show examples where memory is:

- accepted narrowly;
- rejected;
- deferred;
- scoped to project;
- revoked or marked obsolete;
- superseded by a new verified rule.

### Mistake-prevention cases

The strongest Pantheon examples should say:

```text
Here is the mistake raw AI might have helped you make.
Here is how Pantheon slowed the process down.
Here is why that slowdown matters.
```

## Topics to avoid at first

Avoid leading with:

- LangGraph;
- Langfuse;
- GraphRAG;
- agents;
- skills;
- schemas;
- Docker;
- runtime architecture;
- provider routing;
- memory internals.

These are relevant later.

They do not hook a practitioner first.

## Best first demo sequence

Recommended order:

```text
1. Architecture recovery quote and dangerous email.
2. Regulatory watch versus active dossier assumptions.
3. Legal strategy note with missing source and citation verification.
4. Medical referral letter with pseudonymization and clinical decision boundary.
5. Accountant synthesis with unsupported figures.
6. Memory refused because claim is too broad.
```

## Final rule

```text
Do not sell Pantheon as more AI.
Show Pantheon preventing a professional mistake that fluent AI could make easier.
```
